The Hi-Lo system

Every card you see is worth +1, 0, or −1. Keep a running total in your head as the shoe empties — that's your running count.

2 · 3 · 4 · 5 · 6+1
7 · 8 · 90
10 · J · Q · K · A−1
Running count is the raw sum of every card seen since the last shuffle.

True count = running count ÷ decks remaining. With more than one deck in play, the true count is what actually tells you the odds — a +6 running count means very little in an 8-deck shoe, but a lot in a single deck.
Why hide it?

A real table never shows you the count. CountJack hides it too — tap Check Count whenever you want to test yourself. Guess first, then see how close you were.

Where it actually matters

Most of the time the count just shapes how much you'd bet. But one decision at the table uses it directly: insurance. Insurance is only a fair bet when roughly a third of the remaining shoe is 10-value cards — normally the odds are against it, but at a true count of +3 or higher, there are enough tens left that taking it actually pays off. Next time the dealer shows an Ace in Play, check your count first.

Table terms
Hole cards
The 2 private cards only you can see.
Flop / Turn / River
The 3, then 1, then 1 shared community cards dealt face up. Everyone builds their best hand from their hole cards plus these 5.
Check / Bet / Call / Fold
Check passes the action with no money in. Bet puts chips in. Call matches a bet to stay in the hand. Fold gives up the hand and whatever's in the pot.
Kicker
An unpaired card that breaks a tie — e.g. a pair of Kings with an Ace kicker beats a pair of Kings with a Jack kicker.
The nuts
The best possible hand given the current board — unbeatable at that moment.
Open-ended straight draw
4 cards in a row, needing one more on either end to complete — 2 ranks, 8 outs. Example: holding 6-7-8-9 completes with a 5 or a 10.
Gutshot (inside straight draw)
A straight draw missing a card in the middle of the run, not the end — only 1 rank completes it, 4 outs. Example: holding 6-7-9-10 only completes with an 8.
Flush draw
4 cards of the same suit, needing 1 more of that suit to complete the flush — 9 outs.
Position / the button
Acting later in a hand is a real edge — you see everyone else's action first. Heads-up, the button acts first before the flop but last on every street after.
